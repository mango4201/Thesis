\chapter*{Abstract}

The minimum spanning tree (MST) connects all nodes of a network at minimum cost, but those costs are rarely known when the network must be designed. This thesis studies robust spanning trees, fixed in advance and judged against every cost the data allows. It pairs two objectives, min-max and min-max regret, with three uncertainty models: discrete scenarios, per-edge intervals, and a budget capping the total deviation from nominal costs. Each of the six combinations is classified by computational complexity and by the approximation guarantees available.

Under interval costs the upper bounds are worst for every tree at once, so min-max reduces to a single deterministic MST computation. Min-max regret is strongly $\mathsf{NP}$-hard there, and approximable within a factor of $2$. Under a budget min-max stays polynomial, reducing to at most $|E| + 1$ deterministic MST computations, while min-max regret inherits that hardness, which settles the sixth combination. Under discrete scenarios the two objectives behave alike. Both are weakly $\mathsf{NP}$-hard already at two scenarios, and admit pseudo-polynomial algorithms and an FPTAS for constant $K$. Once $K$ is part of the input both turn strongly $\mathsf{NP}$-hard, and neither is approximable within $2 - \varepsilon$ unless $\mathsf{P} = \mathsf{NP}$. They part only over the guarantees available there, a logarithmic factor for min-max against $K$ for regret.

We prove fourteen results in full: the five MST foundations, the interval extremal characterisations, the budgeted reformulation, and both two-scenario reductions. Others, among them the interval hardness and the midpoint guarantee, are developed from their sources in the text; the rest is stated on the authority of the literature. A classification table sets the results side by side, and a fixed four-vertex micro-graph runs through the thesis, closing in a gallery where the two objectives select different trees. A single criterion explains the picture: complexity is set by the cost of making the adversary's worst case independent of the chosen tree.
